## events

Assets: [assets/pngs/hub_events_simple.webp](../assets/pngs/hub_events_simple.webp) (dark) and [assets/pngs/hub_events_simple_light.webp](../assets/pngs/hub_events_simple_light.webp) (light)

Generated on 2026-11-23 with Codex's built-in image generation tool (`image_gen`, Codex CLI 0.171.0, `codex exec` with the references attached by `-i`), at 1536 × 1024. The chosen candidates are unedited tool output; the only processing is the post-processing listed below. Discovery shows them on the full-row Events tile through `HubRowSceneBackdrop`: the picture keeps the right column's width and crop, its left edge fades into the tile.

Dark references: the Feed, News, Reports and Collections dark backgrounds. Three subjects were generated with the same prompt: a tournament chess clock, a demonstration board on its stand, and a pairing bracket. The clock was chosen. It stays legible at 180 × 108 (two big silver dials in one charcoal case, the weight of News' picture block and Feed's play triangle), and it belongs to no neighbour: Smart Events has the board deck and the trophy, Collections the checkered book. The demonstration board's 7 × 7 grid was busy at tile size and close to Collections' checkerboard. The bracket's slots blurred into small shapes, and it came on a backing panel.

Light: the dark clock's own drawing in paper tones, so switching theme changes only the tone, as Collections, News and Feed keep one pose in both themes. The first light drawing (the clock redrawn from scratch in the upper-right half) failed design review: the left button was the raised one, the tilt was gone, the dials were flat-topped ovals and the right dial grazed the edge. It was replaced on the same day.

Light references: the chosen dark clock as Image 1, the subject and pose reference, and the edit target of a recolour where the tool allows it; the Feed, News, Collections and Reports light backgrounds as tone references (Images 2 to 5). Three rounds of three candidates were generated: ink button caps, mid slate caps, and a second, independent take on the ink caps; the same with the dials' measured centres and widths; the same with compass-drawn dials, the third take a fresh drawing rather than a recolour. All nine kept the dark pose: the tilt, the right button raised, the hand angles and pivots, the dial centres within 3 px of the dark ones at 768 × 512 and the right dial 22-24 px clear of the right edge. Every one also repeated the dark drawing's dials, about 3-4% taller than wide. A fourth round edited two of the first round's ink-cap takes with Codex's image tool, with one correction: round dials. Its second candidate, the edit of the first round's second ink-cap take, was chosen first and failed design review: rounding the right dial put the disc 6.5 px lower than the dark's (at 768 × 512) while its hub stayed where the dark's is, so the hub sat 6.8 px above the disc's centre, where the dark's sits on it.

It was replaced by the first round's first ink-cap take, unedited tool output. It has the most centred hubs of the twelve: at 1536 × 1024 the right one is 0.8 px off centre (the next best is 1.7 px) and the left one 6.0 px (the next best is 6.0 px too, the dark's own is 6.6 px). It keeps the dark's dials, 4% taller than wide, so the pair matches dial for dial. Measured at 768 × 512 as the offset of the hub (the densest 13 × 13 px of pale hand and pivot inside the disc) from the disc's bounding-box centre, its hubs sit at (-1.6, -3.1) px left and (-0.1, -0.8) px right. The dark's sit at (-1.1, -3.6) and (-1.0, 0.0). Its dials are 433 × 451 and 450 × 469 px at 1536 × 1024 (the dark's are 430 × 444 and 447 × 465). At 768 × 512 the right dial spans (521, 107) to (744, 340), against the dark's (522, 108) to (744, 339), and sits 23 px clear of the right edge, as on the dark. The button caps are whole, the top one 24 px below the edge, and the case runs off the right edge. The silhouette matches the dark clock's: intersection over union 0.977, dials 0.982. Ink caps beat mid slate: in the dark drawing the caps are as bright as the dials, and as on Feed and News the dark drawing's silver becomes ink on paper.

Post-processing, then 768 × 512 (Lanczos), opaque RGB WebP. Dark, lossy at quality 85: the near-black field (#0a0a0c as generated) is lifted onto the media tile's ink #0c0c0e, fading out by level 28, so charcoal and silver are untouched. The picture then meets the tile's base with no step. Light, lossless like the Feed, News and Reports light drawings, in five steps:

1. A per-channel linear map pins the ink #2a3c42 and moves the generated ground (#e5efef, the median of the empty left third and bottom fifth) to the paper tile's base #eaf2f2 (`lerp(background, surface, 0.45)`).
2. Pixels within 3 levels of that base are set to it exactly.
3. The generator's grain inside the fills is flattened. It cannot be seen at normal contrast (about 1 level, 3 at most for 99% of pixels), but it made the lossless file 116 KB. Fill interiors, found on a 5 × 5 median of the picture, take an edge-preserving average that never mixes two tones. The flat fills (ink #2b4044, case #c8d8d7) are then set to their tone exactly.
4. The soft shading of the back plate, rims and stems stays a smooth ramp, in 1-level steps along its one colour line, so it does not band.
5. Each anti-aliased edge pixel goes on the mixing line of the two fills it sits between, its coverage in steps of at most 5 levels.

No pixel moves more than 6 levels from the mapped picture (0.5 on average). The dials, hubs, buttons and silhouette measure the same before and after the flattening. The file is 12,768 bytes; the light Feed, Reports, Collections and News drawings are 4.0, 4.1, 5.4 and 13.6 KB. Lossless WebP decodes the field as exactly #eaf2f2 (PIL and dwebp agree), so no stripe shows where `HubRowSceneBackdrop` fades the picture's left edge into the tile. The first light drawing was lossy, and its field decoded as #e9f3f2, one level off, which showed as a faint vertical band.
